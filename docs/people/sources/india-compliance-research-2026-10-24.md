## PART A: REGULATORY

### 1. The four Labour Codes

#### 1.1 Commencement
- **[V] All four Codes took effect on 21 November 2025** (Code on Wages 2019, Industrial Relations Code 2020, Code on Social Security 2020, OSH&WC Code 2020). They replace 29 central labour laws.
  - PIB, 21 Nov 2025, PRID 2192463: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2192463&reg=48&lang=2
  - PIB backgrounder, 21 Nov 2025, PRID 2192524: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2192524&reg=3&lang=2
  - PIB, 25 Nov 2025 (before/after table), PRID 2194018: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2194018&reg=48&lang=2
  - EY alert (Nov 2025): https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/alerts-hub/2025/11/new-labour-codes-implemented-across-the-country-effective-21-november-2025.pdf
  - Trilegal, 22 Nov 2025: https://trilegal.com/knowledge-repository/trilegal-update-labour-codes-go-live/
- **[V] The old laws are repealed, except the EPF & MP Act 1952,** which stayed in force on the day (Trilegal). SAM (Oct 2026) adds that the SS Code keeps the old EPF/EPS/EDLI schemes alive for one year from commencement where consistent.
- **[V] Transition:** a Labour Ministry circular says existing rules, regulations, notifications, schemes and standards continue until the new rules replace them. Where a state has not notified its rules, the old state rules still govern in practice (Trilegal; DLA Piper Feb 2026: https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2025/government-of-india-notifies-the-labour-codes-ushers-a-new-era-of-compliances).

#### 1.2 Central rules
- **[V] Draft central rules were published 30 Dec 2025.** The final Central Rules under all four Codes, plus the Model Standing Orders 2026, were **notified on 8 May 2026** and took effect the same day.
  - KPMG GMS Flash Alert 2026-127, 22 May 2026: https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/xx/pdf/2026/05/fa26-127.pdf.coredownload.pdf
  - EY PAS alert, 10 May 2026: https://www.ey.com/en_in/technical/alerts-hub/2026/05/final-rules-published-by-central-government-under-new-labour-codes
  - BDO alert, 12 May 2026: https://www.bdo.in/en-gb/insights/alerts-updates/alert-final-central-rules-notified-under-all-four-labour-codes
  - SCC Online, 13 May 2026 (OSH Central Rules): https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/05/13/osh-central-rules-2026-key-highlights-and-compliance-guide/
- The Central Rules apply only where the Centre is the "appropriate government" (railways, mines, banks, insurance, central PSUs and similar). Most private employers are governed by their **state's** rules.

### 2. DPDP Act 2023 + DPDP Rules 2025

#### 2.1 Dates
- The Act received assent on 11 Aug 2023.
- **[V] The Rules were notified 13 Nov 2025** (G.S.R. series), along with the s.1(2) commencement notification and the Data Protection Board notification.
  - AZB, 14 Nov 2025: https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/
  - SS Rana: https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-notifies-final-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/
  - Deccan Herald (PTI): https://www.deccanherald.com/india/centre-notifies-dpdp-rules-implementation-planned-in-phases-spread-over-12-18-months-3798465
  - KPMG guidance, Nov 2025: https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/dpdp-rules-2025-guidance-to-dpdp-act-implementation.pdf
- **Phased commencement [V]:**
  1. **13 Nov 2025 (immediate):** Act ss.1(2), 2, 18–26, 35, 38–43, 44(1),(3) cover definitions and the Data Protection Board. Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 apply.
  2. **13 Nov 2026 (+12 months; AZB says 12 Nov):** Act s.6(9) and s.27(1)(d), and Rule 4, covering **Consent Manager registration only**.
  3. **13 May 2027 (+18 months; AZB says 12 May):** everything substantive. Act ss.3–5, 6(1)–(8),(10), 7–17, 27 (except (1)(d)), 28–34, 36–37, 44(2). Rules 3, 5–16, 22, 23. This tranche covers notice, consent, legitimate uses, security, breach, retention, rights, children, SDF and penalties.
- **[U] Possible acceleration:** in Jan 2026 MeitY consulted on cutting the 18-month runway to 12 months, which would make 13 Nov 2026 the main deadline. Its other proposals were to start Rule 15 (cross-border), 13(5) and 23 immediately, and Rule 8(3) at 3 months. **No gazette amendment was found as of Oct 2026.** Several Oct 2026 commentaries say 13 May 2027 is still the legal deadline. Sources:
  - https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/
  - https://takshashila.org.in/content/blogs/20260126-meity-considers-reducing-dpdpa-compliance-timelines.html

#### 2.2 Substantive points for an HR system
- **[V] s.7(i) legitimate use for employment:** processing "for the purposes of employment or those related to safeguarding the employer from loss or liability". It covers preventing corporate espionage, protecting trade secrets, IP and classified information, and providing a service or benefit the employee asks for. **No consent is needed.** "Purposes of employment" is not defined, so over-collection (for example family health data or social media) is a risk.
  - Statute text: https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section7.html
  - DLA Piper, 19 Oct 2023: https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/india-key-employer-obligations-under-indias-new-data-protection-regime
- **Notice (s.5 + Rule 3) [V]:** required with every consent request. It must stand alone and be in clear, plain language. It needs an itemised list of personal data, the specific purposes, the goods/services/uses enabled, and a link or means to withdraw consent, exercise rights, and complain to the Board. Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it. English or any 8th-Schedule language. [U] Strictly, s.5 notice is tied to consent, so s.7(i) processing may not legally need it. Best practice is still an employee privacy notice, and benefits-related or optional processing such as wellness, background checks beyond need, or photos for marketing should use consent.
- **Data principal rights [V]:** access to a summary of data and processing (s.11), correction, completion, updating and erasure (s.12), grievance redressal (s.13; Rule 14(3) requires a response **within 90 days**), and nominating someone (s.14). Contact details of the grievance contact or DPO must be published and repeated in every response. [U] Under the Act, ss.11–12 apply to data processed on consent or s.7(a) voluntary provision. Whether they apply to s.7(i) employment processing is arguable.
- **Breach notification (s.8(6) + Rule 7) [V]:**
  - Affected data principals are told **without delay**: nature, extent, timing, location, likely consequences, mitigation, safety steps they can take, and a contact.
  - The **Board gets an initial intimation without delay**, then a **detailed report within 72 hours** of becoming aware (or longer if the Board allows). The report covers facts, cause, mitigation, findings on who caused it, remediation, and the notifications sent to individuals.
  - This runs alongside the separate **CERT-In 6-hour** report.
- **Reasonable security safeguards (s.8(5) + Rule 6) [V]:** at minimum:
  - encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens;
  - access control on computer resources;
  - visibility of access through **logs**, monitoring and review;
  - continued processing after a compromise, such as backups;
  - **retention of logs and personal data for one year** to detect, investigate and remediate unauthorised access (unless law requires otherwise);
  - security clauses in contracts with data processors;
  - technical and organisational measures to monitor all of the above.
- **Retention/erasure (s.8(7)–(8) + Rule 8) [V/U]:** erase when the purpose is served or consent is withdrawn, unless law requires retention.
  - The Third Schedule fixes a **3-year** inactivity erasure period with **48-hour advance notice**, but only for large e-commerce, online gaming and social-media platforms (2 crore or more users). It does not apply to employers.
  - Rule 8(3): personal data, associated traffic data and logs must be kept **at least 1 year** for the Seventh-Schedule purposes (state access).
  - For HR, the practical driver is labour law: registers and returns must be kept for prescribed periods, and payroll and tax records about 8 years under tax law. **[U]** Exact retention periods under the new Code rules need checking per state.
- **Significant Data Fiduciary (s.10 + Rule 13) [V]:** notified by the Centre based on volume, sensitivity and risk. Obligations: an India-based DPO, an independent data auditor, a **DPIA and audit every 12 months**, algorithmic due diligence, and possible data localisation for specified categories. An HR SaaS processor is unlikely to be an SDF itself, but customers who are SDFs will push these duties down by contract.
- **Children (s.9 + Rule 10) [V]:** under 18s need verifiable parental consent, using reliable identity or age details or a DigiLocker-type virtual token. No tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted ads. People with disabilities who have a lawful guardian are handled the same way (Rule 11). HR relevance: dependants' and nominees' data (children's names, DOB, health insurance). Collection is usually on the employee's behalf, but consent design should be considered.
- **Penalties (Schedule) [V/S]:**
  - Up to **₹250 crore**: failing to take reasonable security safeguards.
  - Up to **₹200 crore**: failing to notify a breach; breaching children's obligations.
  - Up to **₹150 crore**: SDF obligations.
  - Up to **₹50 crore**: other breaches.
  - Up to **₹10,000**: data principal duties.
  - Sources: KPMG 2023 decoding (https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/in/pdf/2023/08/decoding-the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023.pdf), IIT BHU summary (https://iitbhu.ac.in/contents/institute/cf/cis/doc/dpdp_act_rules_summary.pdf).
- **Data Protection Board [V]:** constituted Nov 2025, head office in NCR, "digital-first", 4 members at start.

### 3. CERT-In Directions, 28 April 2022 (s.70B(6) IT Act) [V, primary PDF read]
Source: https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf (dated 28 April 2022; effective 60 days after issue, so late June 2022. CERT-In later extended the deadline for MSMEs to 25 Sep 2022 **[U, from memory]**.)
- **Report incidents within 6 hours** of noticing them. Annexure I lists the incident types, including unauthorised access, data breach, data leak, ransomware and identity theft.
- **Clock sync:** connect to the NTP servers of NIC or NPL, or to servers traceable to them. Other time sources must not deviate from NIC/NPL.
- **Logs:** all service providers, intermediaries, data centres, **body corporates** and government organisations must enable logs of all ICT systems. Keep them securely for a **rolling 180 days, within Indian jurisdiction**, and provide them with any incident report or on CERT-In's direction.
- **Point of Contact:** designate one and send CERT-In the details in the Annexure II format.
- (VPN, VPS and cloud providers must keep subscriber KYC for 5 years. This does not apply to an HR SaaS unless it offers those services.)
- Interplay: CERT-In's **180-day log retention** and DPDP Rule 6's **1-year** log retention both apply, so the longer period wins. An HR SaaS hosted in India should keep logs for at least 1 year, in India.

### 4. POSH Act 2013
Sources:
- TaxGuru statutory summary, 15 Feb 2022: https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/posh-2013.html
- Act + Rules PDF (Delhi Police mirror): https://delhipolice.gov.in/doc/POSH/POSH_ActRulesSO.pdf (blocked for download)
- SAM Oct 2026
- PIB SHe-Box doc, Sep 2024: https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2024/sep/doc202493385301.pdf

- **Applies:** every employer with **10 or more employees** must constitute an **Internal Committee (IC)** (s.4). Below 10, complaints go to the district Local Committee.
- **IC composition (s.4(2)) [V]:**
  - Presiding Officer: a senior-level woman employee, or one from another office or unit if none is available.
  - At least 2 employee members committed to women's causes or with social-work or legal experience.
  - 1 external member from an NGO or association, or a person familiar with sexual-harassment issues.
  - At least half the members must be women.
  - Term: no more than 3 years.
- **Complaint (s.9) [V]:** in writing, **within 3 months** of the incident or the last incident in a series. The IC may extend by **3 more months** for recorded reasons.
- **Conciliation (s.10) [V]:** at the complainant's request. No monetary settlement.
- **Inquiry (s.11) [V]:** **complete within 90 days.** The IC has civil-court powers.
- **Report (s.13) [V]:** to the employer **within 10 days** of finishing the inquiry, and shared with both parties. The employer acts **within 60 days**.
- **Interim relief (s.12):** transfer, up to 3 months' leave, or similar.
- **Confidentiality (s.16) [V, statute]:** the identity and addresses of the complainant, respondent and witnesses, the conciliation and inquiry proceedings, the recommendations, and the action taken may not be published or made known to the public, press or media. Breach is penalised under s.17 per the service rules. **Design implication:** strict role-based access, no visibility for ordinary HR or managers, and audit logs.
- **Annual report (s.21 + Rule 14) [V]:** the IC prepares one each calendar year for the employer and the District Officer. It covers complaints received, complaints disposed, cases **pending over 90 days**, workshops and awareness programmes held, and the nature of action taken. **s.22:** the employer includes this in its own annual report (for companies, the Board's report under the Companies (Accounts) Rules).
- **Penalty (s.26):** up to **₹50,000**. Repeat offences carry double the penalty and possible cancellation of licence or registration.
- **SHe-Box [V/S]:** the portal was revamped and relaunched on **29 Aug 2024** (shebox.wcd.gov.in). It holds a central register of ICs and Local Committees, and complainants can file to a chosen IC and track progress. Employers are expected to register their IC, following the SC's *Aureliano Fernandes v. State of Goa* (2023) directions. Delhi issued a notice in June 2025. **Maharashtra's inspection checklist (14 May 2026)** expressly checks SHe-Box onboarding, annual reports, and POSH disclosure in annual filings. **NCW advisory (19 Jun 2026):** mandatory annual POSH audits for 10+ employees. This is a recommendation, not law **[S]**.

### 5. Maternity, gratuity, EPF/ESI thresholds
- **Maternity benefit (now SS Code Chapter VI) [V/S]:**
  - **26 weeks** for the first 2 children, of which up to 8 weeks may be taken before delivery.
  - 12 weeks from the 3rd child onward, and **12 weeks** for adoption of a child under 3 months or for a commissioning mother.
  - Eligibility: **80 days** worked in the 12 months before the expected delivery.
  - Work from home by agreement after the leave.
  - **Crèche** at 50+ employees. The Central SS Rules allow a crèche allowance of at least **₹500/child/month** instead, for up to 2 children.
  - The Central SS Rules list 5 acts of gross misconduct that can forfeit the benefit.
  - Sources: K&S guide (https://ksandk.com/employment-law/guides/social-security-code/), PRS summary (https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2020/Bill%20Summary%20-%20The%20Code%20on%20Social%20Security,%202020_For%20Upload.pdf), KPMG, SAM.
- **Gratuity (SS Code Chapter V) [V]:** 15 days' wages per completed year, using the new "wages" definition. Eligibility is 5 years for permanent staff and 1 year for FTE. Ceiling **₹20 lakh** for private employers (unchanged). Pay within 30 days.
- **EPF wage ceiling: CHANGED [V]**
  - A notification of 29 May 2026 first re-notified the **₹15,000** ceiling under SS Code Chapter III.
  - **S.O. 5109(E) dated 17 Sep 2026** raised it to **₹25,000/month with effect from 17 Sep 2026**, covering EPF, EPS and EDLI.
  - Sep 2026 contributions are split in one ECR: 1–16 Sep at ₹15k, 17–30 Sep at ₹25k. That ECR was due 15 Oct 2026.
  - Existing members earning ₹15k–25k who were outside EPS must be enrolled in EPS.
  - The employer's share may not be shown as an employee deduction through CTC.
  - The new EPF, EPS and EDLI Schemes 2026 (29 Jun 2026) calculate contributions on SS Code "wages". The EPS employer share stays at 8.33%. EDLI benefit is ₹2.5 lakh to ₹7 lakh. EPF interest for FY 2025-26 is 8.25%.
  - Sources: SAM Oct 2026; StudyCafe (https://studycafe.in/epfo-issues-faqs-on-raises-wage-ceiling-from-rs-15-000-to-rs-25-000-over-51-lakh-employees-to-benefit-427171.html); Corporate Professionals 22 Sep 2026 (https://www.corporateprofessionals.com/articles/epf-wage-ceiling-moves-to-%E2%82%B925000-key-changes-and-employer-impact/); BDO (https://www.bdo.in/en-gb/insights/alerts-updates/alert-new-epf-ceiling-hiked).
  - **Any product spec still saying "EPF ceiling ₹15,000" is out of date.**
- **ESI wage ceiling [V/S]:** still **₹21,000/month** (₹25,000 for persons with disability), unchanged since 1 Jan 2017. A rise to ₹25k or ₹30k was discussed at a meeting on 4 Aug 2026, but **nothing has been notified**. ESI now applies pan-India (all districts) under the SS Code. Since Aug 2026, the new IP registration module on ESIC ERP requires Aadhaar validation within 30 days.
  - Sources: https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/esic-wage-ceiling-change-what-it-would-mean-for-employers/ ; https://www.ideasforindia.in/topics/human-development/the-ceiling-for-provident-fund-has-finally-moved-why-not-esi ; SAM.
- **Bonus:** see §1.4 (₹21,000 eligibility / ₹7,000 calculation, retrospective).

### 6. Shops & Establishments, Professional Tax, Labour Welfare Fund

#### 6.1 S&E: state-specific and now converging on the OSH Code [S, SAM Oct 2026]
- **Maharashtra** (Maharashtra S&E (RE&CS) Act 2017):
  - A circular (Apr/May 2026) says **no separate S&E registration for 10+ workers once registered under the OSH Code**.
  - Under 10 workers: still **intimate** commencement (s.7).
  - Other S&E provisions such as hours and leave still apply where consistent.
  - [U] Commonly cited: 9 hrs/day, 48/week, 8 casual leave/yr, earned leave 1 day per 20 days after 240 days (≈18), and carry-forward up to 45 days. Secondary sources conflict.
- **Karnataka** (Karnataka S&CE Act 1961):
  - The **Amendment Act 2026 (4 Sep 2026)** exempts 10+ worker establishments registered under the OSH Code.
  - It also brings lifetime registration, mandatory digital filings, 7 days to notify changes, **service certificates within 7 days**, and a **ban on retaining employees' original documents**.
  - Continue S&E compliance until OSH registration is running in Karnataka.
  - [U] Commonly cited: 9 hrs/day, 48/week, 18 EL after 12 months, 12 sick, 12 casual. National & Festival Holidays Act: 10 holidays.
  - Karnataka **LWF** threshold reportedly cut from 50 to 10 employees in 2025 [S].
- **Tamil Nadu** (TN S&E Act 1947):
  - **365-day operations permission renewed for 3 years from 24 Aug 2026.**
  - Conditions: a weekly holiday on rotation, 8 hrs/day and 48/week, spread-over within 10.5 hrs/day and 57 hrs/week including OT, wages and OT credited to a bank account, written consent for women working 8 pm to 6 am with transport, and an IC under POSH.
  - [U] Commonly cited: 12 days EL, 12 casual/sick.
- **Telangana** (Telangana S&E Act 1988 / Rules 1990, inherited from AP):
  - Register within 30 days. **Deemed registration** if no adverse order within 30 days, and online provisional registration within 24 business hours. Report changes and closure within 15 days. Source: greytHR wiki: https://www.greythr.com/wiki/acts/telangana-shops-commercial-establishments-act/
  - Separate **Telangana Platform-Based Gig Workers Act 2026**, in force 2 Jun 2026.
  - [U] Leave figures not verified.
- Other 2026 moves:
  - Haryana and Andaman & Nicobar exempt OSH-registered establishments.
  - **Bihar repealed** its S&E Act.
  - Assam amended its Act: under 10 workers file an online intimation.
  - Chhattisgarh raised its threshold to 20 workers, 10 hrs/day and 144 hr OT.
  - West Bengal allows 24x7 operation for 20+ employees, 144 hr OT, 8.5 hrs/day.
  - Rajasthan allows year-round opening (10 hrs/day, 48/week, appointment letters required).
- Registration timelines vary: commonly 30 days, 60 days in Maharashtra under the old rules, and 90 days in Delhi [S].
- Overview sources: https://www.india-briefing.com/news/shops-and-establishment-acts-india-an-explainer-33158.html/ ; https://www.legalraasta.com/blog/state-wise-shop-and-establishment-rules

#### 6.2 Professional Tax [S/U]
- A state levy, capped at **₹2,500 per person per year** by Article 276(2) **[V]**. About 20 states and UTs levy it. **Odisha abolished PT from 1 Apr 2026** [S, calcguru].
- **Maharashtra:** ₹200/month, **₹300 in February** (₹2,500/yr). Women are exempt up to ₹25,000/month and men up to ₹7,500 [S].
- **Karnataka:** nil below ₹25,000/month, then ₹200 (₹300 in Feb in some sources). Sources disagree on bands and the annual figure (₹2,400 vs ₹2,500) [U].
- **Telangana:** nil up to ₹15,000, ₹150 for ₹15,001–20,000, ₹200 above [U].
- **Tamil Nadu:** levied **half-yearly** by local bodies (Chennai Corporation etc.) on half-yearly income slabs, and revised periodically [U, not verified this session].
- **States with no PT** include Delhi, Haryana, UP, Rajasthan and others [U].
- Sources: https://calcguru.in/professional-tax-india-state-wise-guide-fy-2026-27/ ; https://hrone.cloud/blog/professional-tax-slab-rates-2026-27/ ; https://tallysolutions.com/accounting/professional-tax-calculation-state-wise-india/
- **Design implication:** a per-state slab table with effective dates, gender-specific slabs (Maharashtra), February overrides, and half-yearly cycles (TN).

#### 6.3 Labour Welfare Fund [S/U]
- About **16 states/UTs**. There is no central Act.
- **Annual** (usually December): AP, Karnataka, TN, Telangana.
- **Half-yearly** (June/December): Chhattisgarh, Delhi, Goa, Gujarat, Kerala (Factories), MP, Maharashtra, Odisha, West Bengal.
- **Monthly:** Chandigarh, Haryana, Kerala (S&E), Punjab.
- Rates **conflict across vendor sources**. Examples: Karnataka ₹50/₹100 vs ₹20/₹40 per year; Maharashtra ₹25/₹75 vs ₹12/₹36 per half-year.
- Source tables: https://ezhrm.in/labour-welfare-fund-india-2026-state-wise-guide/ (claims checked against board notifications, Apr 2026); https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/labour-welfare-fund-india-contribution-rates-due-dates
- Keep rates as dated config, not code.

### 7. Aadhaar: UIDAI restrictions [V, primary PDFs read]
- **UIDAI Circular No. 14 of 2025, dated 4 Nov 2025:** https://www.uidai.gov.in/images/Circular-No.14_of-2025.pdf
  - Supersedes the 25 Jul 2017 ADV circular and Circular 8 of 2025.
  - **Requesting entities** must store Aadhaar numbers and any connected Aadhaar data (eKYC XML etc.) **only in a separate "Aadhaar Data Vault"**.
  - All other systems use a **Reference Key**. **Hashing the Aadhaar number is not allowed as a reference key.** Recovering the number from the key must be computationally infeasible.
  - Encryption: **AES-256 or stronger**. Keys held in an **HSM certified FIPS 140-2 Level 3** or higher.
  - The ADV must sit in a highly restricted network zone, be reachable only through authenticated APIs, have IAM and full access logging, and support secure deletion under the retention policy.
  - Hosting: on-premises, a MeitY-empanelled Government Community Cloud, or ADV-as-a-service. Cloud options need an annual SOC 2 Type II audit by a CERT-In-empanelled auditor.
  - Aadhaar numbers that holders type into authentication requests must not be stored.
- **ADV FAQ v10, 3 Nov 2025:** https://www.uidai.gov.in/images/FAQs_Aadhaar_Data_Vault_03112025_v10.pdf
  - "All REs storing full Aadhaar number… are required to implement" an ADV.
  - Demographics may be stored outside the vault, encrypted, obfuscated or masked, if not linked to the Aadhaar number.
  - The Aadhaar PDF may be stored only inside the ADV.
- **Masking [V]:** UIDAI's masked Aadhaar shows only the **last 4 digits**, with the first 8 replaced by "xxxx-xxxx" (https://www.uidai.gov.in/283-faqs/aadhaar-online-services/e-aadhaar/1887-what-is-masked-aadhaar.html). The RBI KYC Directions also require regulated entities to redact the first 8 digits before storing [S].
- **Statute [U, from statute text, not re-fetched]:**
  - Aadhaar Act s.29(1): **core biometric information** (fingerprints, iris) may not be shared with anyone, nor used except for Aadhaar generation and authentication.
  - s.29(4): no Aadhaar number or core biometric may be published, displayed or posted publicly.
  - Aadhaar (Sharing of Information) Regulations 2016, reg. 6: anyone holding Aadhaar numbers must keep them and any database containing them secure and confidential.
  - *Puttaswamy* (2018) struck down s.57's "contract" route, so private entities cannot **require** Aadhaar except under a law.
- **HR implication and tension:**
  - Notified OSH Rules put the **Aadhaar number in the prescribed appointment-letter format** (SAM).
  - Gig-worker and ESIC registration are also Aadhaar-based.
  - An HR SaaS that is not an AUA/KUA is not strictly a "requesting entity". Even so, the safe design is:
    - store the full Aadhaar only where a law requires it, in an encrypted, isolated vault with a reference key;
    - show only the masked last 4 digits;
    - never store biometrics, including raw templates from biometric attendance devices.
  - Biometric attendance devices should match on-device or use templates that are not Aadhaar core biometrics.

### 8. RPwD Act 2016 and Transgender Persons Act 2019
- **RPwD Act [V/S]:**
  - **s.20:** no discrimination in employment (applies to government establishments). Reasonable accommodation.
  - **s.21:** **every establishment** (private ones included) must notify an **Equal Opportunity Policy** and register a copy with the Chief/State Commissioner. **Rule 8, RPwD Rules 2017** sets the contents: 20+ employees need fuller contents, such as posts suitable for PwDs, training, facilities, accessibility, and a liaison officer; under 20 employees need a lighter version. Publish on the website or display at the premises.
  - **s.23 Grievance Redressal Officer:** the statute applies this to **Government establishments**. The GRO keeps a register of complaints and inquires within 2 weeks of registration. Many private employers appoint one voluntarily, and Rule 8 expects private 20+ establishments to name a liaison officer **[S]**.
  - Sources: https://www.ahlawatassociates.com/blog/equal-opportunity-for-disabled-persons ; https://www.staffnews.in/2018/07/equal-opportunity-policy-appointment-of.html
  - 2026: the Centre widened the "acid attack victim" definition (22 May 2026, SAM).
  - The task's assumption that "s.23 GRO applies to all employers" is **not accurate**. It is a government-establishment duty and good practice for private employers.
- **Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019 [S]:**
  - **s.9:** no establishment may discriminate in recruitment, promotion or related matters.
  - **s.10:** establishments must comply with the Act and provide facilities.
  - **s.11:** every establishment designates a **complaint officer** for complaints of violations.
  - **TP Rules 2020:** Rule 12 requires an equal opportunity policy (infrastructure such as unisex toilets, confidentiality of gender identity, a complaint mechanism). Rule 13 covers the complaint officer, who acts within 15 days.
  - The SC in Oct 2025 directed states and UTs to ensure every establishment designates a complaint officer under s.11 / Rule 13(1).
  - Sources: https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/10/20/supreme-court-suggestions-directions-to-ensure-protection-of-transgender-persons/ ; https://rainmaker.co.in/transgender-persons-protection-of-rights-rules-2020-some-pertinent-points-the-employers-need-to-know/
  - [U] Rule numbers (12 vs 13) and the 15-day timeline are from secondary sources.
- **HR-system implication:** a field for gender identity beyond the binary, kept confidential with restricted access. Configurable grievance channels to route to the POSH IC, the RPwD liaison or GRO, and the TG complaint officer, each with its own access list.

   - Missed punches turn into loss-of-pay deductions and payroll disputes when regularisation lands after the payroll cut-off. Fixes made only in payroll recur the next month.    - Keka: G2 reviewers cite "consistently delayed responses and poor coordination", and chat agents who don't know the product (via HROne).
   - greytHR: slow during peak periods, and support is weaker on payroll rules than on leave (Capterra), https://www.capterra.com/p/150850/greytHR/reviews/
   - Darwinbox: "support team is unhelpful" (G2 5135002 via HROne).
   - Rippling: BBB about 1.4/5 over 27 reviews, mostly about sales, pricing and no phone support, https://www.bbb.org/us/ca/san-francisco/profile/computer-software-developers/rippling-1116-919641/customer-reviews

### B.2 Plain-language naming used by modern tools [V from vendor help docs unless noted]

- **Pattern:** global tools prefer **Time off** to Leave, **People** to Employees, a single **Inbox** or **Approvals** surface, and **Requests** or **Help desk** to Tickets/Grievances. A good hybrid is "Time off" as the module name with "Earned leave / Casual leave / Sick leave" as policy names, because Indian statutes and payslips say "leave".
