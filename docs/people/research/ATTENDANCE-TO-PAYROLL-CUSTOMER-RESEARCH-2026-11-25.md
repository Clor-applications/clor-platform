# When a corrected attendance day still costs someone pay

25 November 2026 · India · Customer evidence, competitor workflows, and Clor source review

**Product thesis.** Make every pay-affecting attendance exception visible before salary approval, and show what happened to it after the cutoff. The strongest starting customer is a payroll lead at an Indian shift-based business with multiple sites or readers, an attendance cutoff, and a recurring list of manual corrections. This is a target to test, with no estimate of market prevalence.

## What Clor can prove from the current source

1. **Inspect the inputs.** The day explanation shows device/browser/import/manual sources, recorded punches, trust signals, and how the day was computed. Month Confirmations provides an HR workflow to review and answer worker disputes. [DayExplain](../../../apps/attendance/src/components/DayExplain.jsx#L34) · [MonthConfirmations](../../../apps/attendance/src/screens/MonthConfirmations.jsx#L18)
2. **Show the selected version of attendance.** Payroll reads saved snapshots for eligible closed months and identifies live figures as provisional. If a stored closed month is reopened or superseded by a new snapshot, approval or lock rejects stale inputs and instructs the operator to take the figures again. Locking when loss-of-pay values disagree across sources requires an explicit reason. [Attendance snapshots](../../../server/src/services/payroll/attendanceSnapshots.ts#L44) · [Pay-run approval and lock](../../../server/src/routes/payrollRuns.ts#L1199) · [Guard test cases](../../../server/src/__tests__/payrollGuards.test.ts#L74)
3. **Reconcile a specific early-payroll workflow.** With its provisional-days setting enabled, Clor can account for days beyond the cutoff, reconcile actual attendance later, and apply a correction to a subsequent payroll. The suite includes a payback example and a test that a later pull does not duplicate a correction already consumed. This path has explicit setup and prerequisites. [Provisional days setting](../../../apps/payroll/src/components/ProvisionalDaysSetting.jsx#L66) · [Correction test cases](../../../server/src/__tests__/payrollProvisionalDays.test.ts#L180)
4. **The remaining product gap is a single place to see the whole case.** Source review found related day, dispute, cutoff and pay-run screens. It did not establish a single current screen that shows one employee-date exception, the estimated rupee effect, the approver, the pay-run version and the eventual correction payment together. The source tests were inspected as evidence of intended behavior and were not executed for this research.

## The candidate advantage and how to test it

**The job:** “Before I approve payroll, show me which people could be paid incorrectly, why, and what I can do about it. If a correction arrives after cutoff, tell me which pay run will settle it.”

**First workflow to prove:** One list ordered by pay impact or deadline. Open a missed checkout to see its source, shift rule, original and corrected days, who approved it, and its pay effect. Give the payroll lead a clear disposition such as *applied in this run*, *waiting for manager*, *recalculate before approval*, or *scheduled for next run*. Show employees a practical explanation and safe way to dispute their own record. A rupee amount should state whether it is estimated, calculated or paid. All labels here describe proposed behavior.

For a concrete benchmark, give each operator the same three cases:

- **Before cutoff.** Missing checkout and approved half-day leave on the same date. Resolve the day and reconcile the payroll input.
- **After calculation, before approval.** Reopen and correct a previously closed attendance month. Find the now-stale pay run and safely refresh it.
- **After payment.** A supported late correction changes the prior calculation. Locate the exact delta, confirm which run will carry it, and show the worker a reason.

Measure active minutes, screen and tool switches, manual re-entry, whether the operator catches stale inputs, and whether the worker can understand the result unaided. Ask shift-based HR leads and payroll processors to bring a de-identified recent close and walk one exception from clock record to payment. Validate against at least Keka and greytHR under the same configured policy. If the same job is already easy in those products, the comparison should narrow to a specific organization profile or installation constraint. The value claim needs observed task performance.

**The Sahi analogy.** Sahi documents placing and adjusting orders directly from a chart. Clor’s comparable demonstration is to open a missed punch, see what it would cost in payroll, make the decision with the evidence in view, and show which pay run reflects it.
