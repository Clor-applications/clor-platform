# Clor home pages · Content and layout research

25 November 2026 · Admin Center, People, Ops, Payroll and Attendance

Each home page should answer one operational question, show the few numbers needed to make that decision, and open the work already filtered to the right scope. The proposed priorities below come from Clor source, official competitor workflows and the [prior attendance-to-payroll customer review study](../people/research/ATTENDANCE-TO-PAYROLL-CUSTOMER-RESEARCH-2026-11-25.md). These are workflow and product benchmarks. Exact deadlines, frequency of pain and time savings still need validation with Clor users. The visual directions use fictional data and show an authorized working role for each app.

## Admin Center · What needs an account owner today?

**First screen.** Lead with account access at risk, overdue billing that can affect service, or a company-wide setup blocker. Include two to four values as applicable: overdue invoice count and amount, available seats out of plan capacity, invitations awaiting acceptance with an ageing breakdown when available, and privileged users missing an authenticator if policy requires it. Show company/account scope with each value. For a scoped admin, lead with the companies and access tasks they manage. A regular member needs app launch and access-request status.

**Notifications and actions.** Prioritize a service-impacting plan state, a seat limit blocking a joiner, and privileged access risk. Put external access expiring soon and admin-owned sync errors in the queue with a specific expiry or source. State which issues Clor is handling. Show routine invites and recent changes in secondary activity. Deep links: Billing & seats → affected invoice, Members → missing-authenticator filter, Invitations → pending, Outside Access → expiring, Companies → affected setup, Apps → open or manage an assigned app. The current sync warning has no action in the home source, so a direct link to a diagnostic view is a proposed improvement.

**Evidence.** The [Admin home](../../apps/admin/src/screens/AdminHome.jsx) already has account/plan state, billing, pending invitations, seat warnings, outside-access expiry, privileged users missing an authenticator and data-health issues. Microsoft 365 documents the distinction between issues an organization can fix and service incidents handled by the provider. [Microsoft 365 Service health](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/enterprise/view-service-health?view=o365-worldwide)

## People · Whose request or employment change is waiting on me?

**First screen.** A manager needs approvals assigned to them, requests due or starting today, and who is out in their direct team today. HR needs due and overdue onboarding or exit tasks where they exist, probation decisions approaching a configured deadline, and unresolved requests by owner or stage. Each employee needs the next shift or today's attendance state, actionable request updates, and relevant available leave balance with the policy year. Team or company headcount belongs in a secondary context panel unless an upcoming joiner or exit changes what someone must do this week.

**Notifications and actions.** Surface a leave request that starts today, an overdue probation decision, or an onboarding task that blocks a start date. Make approver, request start date and decision deadline distinct. Show outcomes and upcoming holidays as informational. Links: Approvals → assigned queue or particular request, My Requests → selected request, My Leave → balance or new request, employee register → employee and lifecycle milestone, and Attendance → month close only when that task is relevant to the viewer. Use counts of requests for approval work and unique people when describing team availability.

**Evidence.** [People Overview](../../apps/people/src/screens/PeopleOverview.jsx) and [Home Today](../../apps/people/src/screens/HomeToday.jsx) expose waiting work, upcoming probation, team week, self-serve information and Attendance context. Reliable organization-wide onboarding/exit blocker totals with assignees and deadlines need coverage verified before displaying them. Zoho People uses distinct employee and team views and surfaces pending actions alongside personal attendance/leave. [Zoho People My Space](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/home-tab/articles/my-space)

## Ops · What customer impact needs an owner right now?

**First screen.** Lead with active customer-impacting incidents, affected customers or sites when measured, and urgent unassigned work. Show time since detection or last update. For support show assigned and unassigned cases near a documented first-response deadline. Finance sees failed collection attempts and overdue accounts in explicit windows. Security sees open incidents within its access scope. Keep platform service health distinct from the support, billing and customer lifecycle queues.

**Notifications and actions.** Escalate a confirmed customer-impacting outage, a customer locked out or a configured response deadline about to be missed. Group correlated failures under the incident, show ownership and recent status, and avoid repeating a notification on every poll. Links: Health → affected service, support queue → priority/unassigned, Customers → affected account, Payments → failed collection, Email log → failed delivery. Leave capacity and internal telemetry in Health unless they explain customer impact or a clear next action.

**Evidence.** [Ops Home](../../packages/ops/Home.jsx) already consumes summary and service-health data, shows access-dependent work, and supports claiming and deferring items. The [Ops API](../../packages/ops/api.js) and [Ops routes](../../server/src/routes/ops.ts) underpin these surfaces. Authoritative incident impact counts, response clocks and incident correlation require verified data and rules. Atlassian documents queue ordering by time left on a measured response goal. Google SRE recommends paging for actionable problems tied to service symptoms. [Jira Service Management queues](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-service-management-cloud/docs/what-are-queues/) · [Google SRE monitoring](https://sre.google/sre-book/monitoring-distributed-systems/)

## Payroll · What stands between this run and paying people accurately?

**First screen.** Show pay period, company/pay group where configured, run stage, next accountable role and confirmed cutoff. Give blockers the highest priority: missing pay or bank data as applicable, stale or missing attendance input, unresolved pay-affecting corrections and approval gating. Keep separate values for employees in run, current net payable and variance vs the last comparable finalized run. Show both rupees and percentage when the comparison is valid. Allow preparers to inspect changed input, and approvers to see status, variance and evidence. A draft total needs a visible label.

**Notifications and actions.** A newly stale attendance snapshot, a returned approval, or a blocker approaching pay cutoff merits a targeted notification. Show which snapshot the run consumed, whether a correction has been included, and who can act. Links: Payroll Runs → exact run and stage, Attendance Payroll Readiness → same month and matching scope, salary revisions/adjustments → relevant pending work, approval → run review. Mark approved, locked, posted and payment released as separate states where there is a data source for each.

**Evidence.** [Payroll Overview](../../apps/payroll/src/screens/PayrollOverview.jsx) and its [overview API](../../server/src/routes/payrollOverview.ts) expose current period, run stage and amounts, employee count, prior runs, pending inputs and identified blockers. [Payroll run routes](../../server/src/routes/payrollRuns.ts) implement run-time checks. Attendance snapshot and loss-of-pay reconciliation are verified in the earlier study. A single employee/date → approved correction → exact included pay delta view is proposed and needs reconciliation data and permissions. Keka documents payroll controls while maker-checker approval is pending. greytHR documents syncing attendance updates into processed payroll. [Keka payroll finalisation](https://help.keka.com/hc/en-us/articles/41388353533073-Approval-Workflow-Maker-Checker-for-Payroll-Finalisation) · [greytHR loss-of-pay days](https://www.greythr.com/help-admin/payroll/manage-employee-lop-days/)

## Attendance · Is this shift covered, and what could prevent a clean close?

**First screen.** During the day show checked in out of people due to start by now, no check-in past the applicable grace period, unresolved exceptions, and affected readers or sites. Show the site/shift, timezone and source freshness. Exclude future shifts, approved leave and off-days from expected-on-shift counts as applicable. Count unique people for coverage and count records separately for correction work. Near month close, promote outstanding pay-affecting blockers, confirmation/dispute status and the month snapshot state. Managers see their team. An attendance operator sees authorized sites and unresolved punches.

**Notifications and actions.** A site reader outage affecting the current shift, a missing punch that survives policy grace, and a payroll-close blocker need different routing. A missing punch is a provisional exception until the source is healthy and policy is evaluated. Links: Exceptions → site, shift and dates; Requests → pending corrections; Reader Health → affected site/reader; Month Close → month; Month Confirmations → unresolved responses; Payroll Readiness → exact readiness issue where supported. Routine late/leave activity stays in its filtered view.

**Evidence.** [Attendance Today](../../apps/attendance/src/screens/AttendanceToday.jsx) has current-day counts, corrections and reader health. [Month Close](../../apps/attendance/src/screens/MonthClose.jsx), [Month Confirmations](../../apps/attendance/src/screens/MonthConfirmations.jsx) and [Payroll Readiness](../../apps/attendance/src/screens/PayrollReadiness.jsx) supply month workflow views. Verify the shift denominator, payroll-impact classification and source freshness per site before claiming those figures. UKG gives managers access to timecard exceptions and documents the distinction between submitted and applied edits. [UKG Pro Workforce Management](https://communityfiles.ukg.com/support/KOL/OnlineHelp-WorkforceDimensions/en-us/Content/Timekeeping/MyTimeStamp.htm)

## What firsthand reports change

- A Zoho People reviewer values seeing leave balance and upcoming leave, while describing repetitive navigation. Another reports cross-module clicks and limited mobile manager actions. The design should put the decision and the exact request on the same path and make that path usable on a phone. [Zoho People reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/zoho-people/reviews)
- A Keka reviewer describes missing punches when a clock has network trouble and payroll discrepancies that required support. Other reviewers value one-click leave and attendance. This supports testing a visible source-health indicator, a trustworthy punch receipt, and traceable pay changes as distinct improvements. [Keka reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/149253/Keka/reviews/)
- A public UKG rollout guide from the University of New Mexico shows a manager view split into timecard issues that must be fixed, potential issues requiring review, and clean timecards. This makes the home-page split between blockers and discretionary review a workflow benchmark. [University of New Mexico UKG job aid, p. 45](https://time.unm.edu/resources/job-aids_ukg_v1.pdf#page=45)

## Shared dashboard behavior

- Put the next decision, 2–4 supporting values, and the next work link in the first viewport wherever the screen permits. Set a clear period and scope. Put volume trends, activity, and chart history below the decision unless they change what should happen next.
- Use three urgency levels. **Blocking** means a decision or scheduled outcome cannot progress. **Due soon** means someone has an actionable deadline. **Update** means the viewer needs context, usually through activity or a digest. Order by operational impact, then deadline and age. Each action row gives a specific record or cohort, impact, owner, due time when configured, and a route.
- Display zero only after a successful scoped response. Show an explicit stale, partial, unavailable or no-access state where appropriate. Use the same access checks for totals, labels and drill-down as for underlying records. Keep compensation and customer incident detail scoped.
- Preserve company, site/team, period and relevant filters on links where supported. Treat deeper links and missing owner, cutoffs, freshness, pay-delta or SLA measurements as separate data requirements when the current source cannot supply them.
- Show one source of action for cross-app issues. For an attendance correction, Attendance owns the record. Payroll warns when the run is blocked and links back to that record or its filtered readiness view. Suppress duplicate notifications unless responsibility or severity changes.

## Layout directions to compare

- **Action inbox** opens on a ranked task list with short impact, owner, due time and action. A narrow companion area holds figures. It favors rapid triage.
- **Status board** opens on four equally readable measures, then exceptions and work links. It favors repeated health checks. Supporting figures need context and should never outshine a blocker.
- **Run of day** opens on ordered checkpoints with an active step and upcoming deadlines. It favors shifts and pay-cycle handoffs. Use explicit unknown timing when no schedule is configured.
- **By team or site** opens on a scope comparison with a selectable detail panel. It favors people who oversee multiple teams, sites, companies, service queues or pay groups. Only authorized scopes appear.

Compare the four with the same data. Check whether a manager can find the approval starting today, an operator can find the affected site, and a payroll lead can prove whether a correction made it into the current run. Measure time to the right record, detours and confidence in the status. The last payroll task depends on the evidence proposed above.

## Space and layout plan by page

Size controls by their labels, lists by the information they need to display, and tables by the columns people compare. Give additional width to content that benefits from it, such as record titles, explanatory text, timelines and side-by-side evidence. Let short sections end at their last row. Extra width is useful only if it improves scanning or supports a decision.

| Page | Where the width goes | Compact information | Narrow screens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **People Home** | An action column for personal actions, approvals and news, with room for who, what and when on each row. | A team and calendar column for overview, events, leave and celebrations. Quick Launch wraps as a toolbar of content-sized buttons. | Put the action alert, shortcuts, personal actions and assigned work first. Stack the team and calendar sections. |
| **Admin Center Home** | An account action list with company or member, consequence and next step. Give searches and access-review tables room for names, roles and dates. | Plan status, seat capacity, guest access expiry and billing totals sit together in an account summary. | Lead with account risks and actions. Put account figures in a short list and maintain company context on drill-down. |
| **Ops Home** | A triage table has room for customer or service, impact, owner and time. Selected incidents can use an adjacent evidence panel when both stay readable. | A narrow area holds active incident totals and service health. Keep telemetry in Health unless it explains an open incident. | Show severity, impacted customer and owner in each queue row. Open incident detail on its own screen. |
| **Payroll Home** | Give the current run and blocker list enough width for issue, employee or group, responsible role and cutoff. Let variance and reconciliation tables span the available width when showing them. | Put run stage, employee count, draft net and next cutoff in a compact summary with clear labels. | Put the cutoff and blockers above run figures. Show employee-level evidence on the selected run or case screen. |
| **Attendance Home** | Give site and shift coverage, the roster and exception details the main space. Use a wider table only when site, shift and exception columns help comparison. | Keep expected versus checked in, reader freshness and month-close state as short readouts. | Use site and shift context above the exception list. Keep freshness visible so missing punches stay interpretable. |

On list pages, keep search and filters near the records, give the record name the flexible column, align figures, and keep statuses and actions compact. Use row detail for long notes and optional columns where data comparisons support a task. On profile and record pages, keep the record content in the main column and use a side column for related actions only when there are enough actions to justify it.

People is the first implementation. Its layout switches using the available content width after navigation. At roughly 752 px of workspace width it uses two independently flowing columns, with about 35% for the team and calendar column. Below that, sections stack and shortcuts wrap. Verify populated, sparse and restricted views on actual desktop and phone widths before copying any pattern elsewhere.
